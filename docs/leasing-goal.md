# Atrium leasing-first goal

Accepted by the owner on September 22, 2026, following the leasing advisor meeting. This is the current product priority; it does not assert that these capabilities are delivered. The shared task board remains the ownership index.

Build Atrium into a reliable AI leasing platform for owners/operators of roughly 50–500-unit multifamily buildings. Deliver accurate answers, completed tours, fast lead response and reduced staff work through an impressive mobile-friendly demo and controlled pilot. Preserve a maintainable multi-tenant foundation for the broader scope of work.

1. Complete leasing: inbound questions, qualification, availability, booking, rescheduling, cancellations, permissioned confirmations/follow-ups and contextual staff handoff. Integrate existing websites. Target consented website-triggered callback initiation within about 15 seconds when operating hours and provider readiness permit. Distinguish confirmed actions from pending requests. Phase broader resident/maintenance operations after leasing reliability; select PMS adapters when pilot customers' systems are known.

2. Learn from approximately 100 representative, permissioned leasing recordings/transcripts. Protect personal information; categorize intents, questions, escalations and outcomes. Support human-assisted discovery if needed while engineering continues. Keep held-out evaluations separate from prompt development; distinguish synthetic tests from real-call evidence.

3. Use authoritative structured inventory, prices and calendars, approved policy knowledge, source/freshness tracking and ingestion outside live conversations where practical. Never invent missing facts. Enforce property staffing capacity, unit blackouts, time zones and booking policies. Make writes, notifications and retries durable and resistant to duplicates, with recovery for uncertain/partial outcomes.

4. Measure voice latency across endpointing, transcription, model, tools, speech and network, including median/tail performance over actual phone audio. Benchmark Vapi and alternatives before switching. Compare accuracy, action reliability, naturalness, speed, concurrency and cost per successful outcome. Treat meeting vendor/model suggestions as hypotheses; avoid premature rewrites or GPU commitments. Use transparent AI disclosure and licensed/consented voices. Test interruptions, pronunciation, corrections and long conversations.

5. Handle known-unit inquiries directly. Progressively qualify broader searches, normally shortlist up to five relevant matches and present a few aloud with clear coverage. Offer permissioned links to matches, remember supplied details and preserve handoff context.

6. Deliver a polished navy/blue-and-white dashboard with accessible mobile layouts, restrained motion, calls/leads/tours, manual rescheduling, unit blocks, unit feedback, follow-ups and exception resolution. Demo visuals must reflect actual state. Preserve tenant isolation, roles, audit trails, observability, recovery and modular integrations.

Acceptance: Target >=95% end-to-end success on a defined representative held-out routine-leasing evaluation; report sample size, scope and failures. Evaluate 80–90% eligible routine-inquiry containment as a pilot target without suppressing necessary escalation. All defined critical isolation, authorization, duplicate-action, false-confirmation and data-loss tests must pass. Verify phone-to-tool-to-dashboard actions, notification delivery, degraded services and concurrency. Establish latency/cost budgets from baselines. Report local, sandbox and production evidence separately.

Delivery: Sequence discovery/baseline, data/workflow reliability, voice optimization/website lead capture, controlled pilot, then broader SOW expansion. Protect live demos from unverified changes. Provide daily EOD reports and reciprocal Codex/Fable handoffs with changes, commits/deployments, checks, limitations, owner to-dos and next-agent to-dos. Owner inputs include permissioned call samples, verified property rules, pilot access and service funding. Continue independent work while inputs are pending. Measure progress by accepted customer outcomes.

## Evidence boundaries

The meeting's provider recommendations and reported accuracy are hypotheses, not independently verified benchmarks. Current synthetic tests cannot establish real-call success, latency or containment. A representative approximately 100-call corpus has not yet been assembled for this evaluation; the supplied incident transcript is a useful regression example. Obtain permissioned, deidentified examples before using real calls for development; keep held-out cases separate.

The original broader scope of work remains a phased roadmap. Existing resident and maintenance foundations are preserved. Automated message delivery, website callbacks, customer onboarding, production tenant activation and PMS integrations require their own verified end-to-end acceptance.
